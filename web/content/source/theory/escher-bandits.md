# The Escher-Bandit

In the traditional multi-armed bandit (MAB) framework, exploration is often visualized as a series of independent trials. However, the *Insulindian Miracle* project posits that institutional exploration is not merely a statistical exercise but a **spatial and structural transformation**. 

The "Heerich Voxel" aesthetic represents this transformation. Just as Erwin Heerich used geometric cardboard structures to explore the additive and subtractive logic of space, a bandit policy explores the reward space of a peninsula.

## Impossible Geometries of Regret

Regret, in bandit theory, is the difference between the reward of the optimal action and the action taken. In an "Escher-Bandit" scenario, the reward surface is not a flat landscape but a **recursive, non-Euclidean loop**.

- **The Penrose Staircase of Institutional Drift:** A policy might find itself in a loop where every "improvement" in resource extraction leads back to a state of institutional decay, much like the impossible ascending/descending stairs in Escher's *Klimmen en dalen*.
- **Isometric Ambiguity:** In isometric projection, a cube's position in 3D space can be ambiguous. Similarly, a high immediate reward in a "boomtown" site might be ambiguous: is it a signal of long-term prosperity or a deceptive peak in a rotting resource cycle?

## Voxelized Decision Spaces

By visualizing the decision space as a 3D voxel structure, we emphasize that every choice builds upon the last. The structure is not just a path; it is an **architecture of decisions**.

1. **The Core Column:** Represents the exploitation of known, stable rewards.
2. **The Branching Arms:** Represent explorations into high-variance terrain.
3. **The Escher Loops:** Represent the "Megacity Trap" or "Resource Curse," where exploration leads into structural dead-ends that appear locally optimal but are globally catastrophic.

The temporal playback of our data storytelling mimics the construction of this architecture—iteration by iteration, the "Miracle" (or the "Mirage") reveals its true geometric form.
